# ISO 3166-1 — the eight countries RegOS seeds

**What this records:** the alpha-3 codes and official English short names for
the eight jurisdictions in the demonstration seed, and — more importantly —
**what RegOS does and does not hold.**

## What is held

| | |
|---|---|
| **The register itself** | ❌ **not held.** ISO publishes 3166-1; the machine-readable register is licensed |
| **These eight rows** | ✅ hand-verified against ISO's freely published Online Browsing Platform |

**Level 3** — the authority's published listing, read. Not 2a: nothing
machine-readable was parsed, and no third party checked the result.

## The eight

| Alpha-2 | Alpha-3 | Common name (screen) | ISO 3166-1 English short name |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | USA | United States | United States of America |
| CA | CAN | Canada | Canada |
| GB | GBR | United Kingdom | United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland |
| DE | DEU | Germany | Germany |
| FR | FRA | France | France |
| JP | JPN | Japan | Japan |
| AU | AUS | Australia | Australia |
| IN | IND | India | India |

## Why both codes and both names are stored

**Neither pair is derivable from the other, in either direction.**

`DE → DEU` looks mechanical and `GB → GBR` less so, but the alpha-2 and alpha-3
registers are assigned separately and elsewhere no rule connects the pair at all.
The same is true of the names: *"United Kingdom"* is what a person says and
*"United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland"* is what the register
says, and there is no transformation between them.

Six of these eight have an ISO name identical to the common name, which is
exactly the trap — a rule inferred from six rows would be wrong on the other two,
and would be wrong silently, in a submission.

## What would change this

| If this turns out wrong | What breaks |
|---|---|
| An alpha-3 code is wrong | Every xEVMPD/IDMP message naming that country (EPIC-007b), silently — the message stays well-formed |
| An ISO name is wrong | The same, wherever the official name is required rather than the common one |
| The seed widens past eight | **This entry stops covering it.** Eight hand-verified rows and a register are different evidence levels, and only one can be widened without fetching something |

## The distinction this entry exists to protect

RegOS has been caught once by recording *"we know there is a vocabulary"* as
*"we hold the vocabulary"* — the `file-tag` correction (E29 → E33). So, stated
plainly:

> **RegOS does not hold ISO 3166-1.** It holds eight rows a person checked, in a
> seed file that says so in its own docstring, for a demonstration.

The moment a ninth country is needed, someone fetches the value. That is the
cost of not holding a register, and it is the honest cost.
